How it works

Furniture returns are expensive and most come down to one question: will it fit, and will it look right? Snug answers it in three steps.

  1. 1

    Scan your room

    Tap the room's corners through the camera. ARKit turns them into walls, doors and windows. Pro iPhones can use LiDAR, but you don't need it.

  2. 2

    Place real products

    Pick from real Amazon listings. Each one drops into a 3D model of your room at its listed size, in its true colors.

  3. 3

    Know before you buy

    Every piece gets a fit verdict against your walls, doors and other furniture. Then buy the whole room in one list.

An honest fit check

A phone scan isn't a laser. Snug knows its measurements can be off by a few centimeters, so it never shows a green check it can't back up. Every verdict accounts for a ±5 cm uncertainty band.

  • Fits with room to spareClear by more than twice the margin.
  • FitsClear by more than the margin.
  • Too close to callWithin the margin. Snug tells you which wall to measure.
  • Won't fitOverlaps by more than the margin.

Engineering

A native iOS app built end to end: capture, ML, geometry, rendering and the asset pipeline.

AR capture on any iPhone

Corner tapping on ARKit world tracking, with a floor baseline and projection for walls taller than the camera can see. Results go into one room model shared by both capture paths (corner tapping and LiDAR).

On-device furniture detection

A YOLO model fine-tuned on merged open datasets to detect 11 furniture classes at 0.74 mAP50. It runs on the phone with CoreML, so the furniture you already own shows up in your scan.

Fit engine

Separating-axis collision on rotated footprints, plus clearance to walls and doorways, classified against the uncertainty band. It's pure, deterministic and covered by Swift Testing.

True-to-life 3D

A RealityKit diorama under neutral white light, so colors match what arrives. An offscreen renderer makes the room thumbnails and before/after share images.

3D asset pipeline

Mesh simplification and compression cut the product models from 682 MB to 28 MB, small enough to download over cellular.

Built to last

SwiftUI with MVVM, SwiftData with versioned schema migrations, no third-party dependencies in the app, zero compiler warnings, and CI on GitHub Actions.
